  Module 4 â€” Supply Planning (Factory, Warehouse & Raw Materials)
4.1  The Problem It Solves
Currently, the S&OP system generates a demand signal â€” tertiary forecast, derived secondary and primary volumes. But this signal does not connect to production. The factory team works from their own plans. The result is the classic disconnect: S&OP says demand will spike in week 38, but the production schedule was set 6 weeks ago based on last quarter's actuals.
4.2  What We Build â€” The 5M Framework
Supply planning in manufacturing is built around five resources â€” the 5Ms. We design a module for each:
4.2.1  Manpower Planning
Translate the weekly primary volume plan into a shift plan. If primary volume in week 38 is 40,000 units and line capacity is 2,000 units per shift, the system tells you: you need 20 shifts that week.
Factor in planned leaves, public holidays (which we already have in week_master), and shift patterns. Flag if required shifts exceed available workforce capacity.
Output: weekly headcount requirement vs. available capacity â€” one line per assembly line or product family.
4.2.2  Machine / Capacity Planning
Define capacity master: each assembly line, its rated throughput per shift, and current planned downtime (maintenance, changeovers).
Map the primary volume plan to machine capacity â€” produces a weekly utilisation % per line.
If utilisation exceeds 85% (a standard planning threshold), the system raises a capacity alert and suggests either overtime, subcontracting, or demand-side adjustment.
4.2.3  Material Planning (Bill of Materials linkage)
For each model code, define a simplified BOM (Bill of Materials): screen, battery, PCB, casing, charger, box. These are the critical long-lead components.
Multiply the weekly primary plan by the BOM to get material requirements by week. Compare against procurement lead times (local: 2 weeks; overseas: 8-12 weeks) to produce a buy signal.
Differentiate in-house vs outsourced components. For outsourced parts (e.g. screens from a contract manufacturer), the buy signal goes to procurement with a due-date.
Example output: 'For SKU Lava X3, week 40 primary plan is 12,000 units. You need 12,000 screens. Lead time is 6 weeks. Place PO by week 34.'
4.2.4  Warehouse & Storage Planning
Project finished goods inventory by week at each warehouse hub, using the same stock roll-forward logic we already have.
Flag warehouses that will exceed rated capacity (in units or cubic metres) â€” allows logistics team to plan inter-warehouse transfers or temporary storage in advance.
Show inbound materials flow (components arriving) vs. outbound dispatch schedule â€” so the warehouse team can plan dock slots and manpower.
4.2.5  Manufacturing Schedule (Order Freeze linkage)
Once orders are frozen (Module 2), the system generates a tentative production schedule: which model on which line in which week.
The schedule is shown as a Gantt-style view by product family. Planning can drag-and-drop to adjust sequencing while the system validates capacity constraints in real time.
Changeover penalties (time lost switching from one model to another) can be configured and are factored into capacity utilisation.

  Module 6 â€” Consensus Flow
6.1  What Consensus Flow Means in S&OP
In a full S&OP process, the word 'consensus' has a precise meaning: it is the single agreed forecast number that all functions â€” Sales, Supply Chain, Finance, and Leadership â€” commit to for a given time horizon. Without a structured consensus process, each function defends their own number, meetings go in circles, and the 'final' plan is whatever the most senior person says at the end of the meeting.
6.2  What We Build
The Demand Review Step
Sales and Marketing review the statistical demand forecast from our S&OP engine. They can propose adjustments â€” called 'demand overrides' â€” for known business events: a product launch, a competitor's product exit, a promotional push.
Each override must have a reason code (new distribution, promotion, competitor, market intelligence) and an expiry week (after which the override lapses unless renewed).
The system shows the statistical forecast alongside the override in the same table, with the % deviation highlighted. This prevents overriding by gut feel alone.
The Supply Review Step
Supply chain reviews constrained supply: if factory capacity or material availability limits primary dispatch below the demand plan, this is flagged as a supply gap.
The supply review produces a Supply Constrained Plan: the demand plan capped at what supply can realistically deliver. The gap between unconstrained demand and supply constrained plan is made visible â€” not hidden.
The Pre-S&OP and Executive S&OP Steps
Pre-S&OP: The planning team consolidates the demand override, supply constraint, and financial view into a single consensus pack. Unresolved issues are listed as escalations.
Executive S&OP: Leadership reviews the consensus pack, resolves escalations, and ratifies the final plan. The ratified plan is saved as the official version in the system (write to weekly_forecast with a consensus flag).
Version Control and Audit
Every plan version is time-stamped with the user who submitted or approved it. You can replay the history of a forecast: what was the plan on week 30, what was it on week 35, and who changed it and why.
Deviation reports: compare the consensus plan from 4 weeks ago vs. today. This is the input to forecast accuracy improvement meetings.
Process Change, Not Just Technology
The consensus module is as much about process design as software. When pitching this to Lava leadership, it is important to say: we will work with you to design the meeting cadence, the roles (Demand Manager, Supply Planner, S&OP Manager), and the escalation rules.
The software enforces the process â€” you cannot skip a step, you cannot publish a plan without supply review, and all changes are logged. This is governance that a spreadsheet cannot provide.

7.3  Seasonality â€” Monthly Patterns
How We Model It
We already have a seasonality collection in the database. The current implementation uses it as a scalar. We extend this to a proper seasonality decomposition.
For each SKU (or family), compute a 52-week seasonal index using 2+ years of historical tertiary data. Index = (actual that week) / (average week). Values above 1.0 are high season; below 1.0 are low season.
The index is applied as a multiplier on the base forecast. If week 43 has a seasonal index of 1.35 (Diwali season), the base forecast is scaled up by 35%.
Example: Lava Blaze, mature stage, base weekly forecast 8,000 units. Week 43 seasonal index 1.35. Adjusted forecast: 10,800 units. Secondary and primary derived accordingly.
Key Seasonal Patterns for Lava to Configure
Diwali / Navratri season (September-October): typically 25-40% uplift for entry-level and mid-range smartphones.
Year-end clearance (December-January): retailer destocking, slower consumer demand â€” often a 15-20% dip.
Budget phone seasonality differs from premium: rural Tier 3 buyers respond to harvest season cash (October, March) while urban buyers respond to festive gifting.
Regional patterns: North India has a different festive calendar than South India. Location-based indices (next section) allow this to be modelled at state level.
7.4  Promotions & Trade Schemes
What We Build
A promotion calendar: for each SKU, in each week, is there an active promotion? If yes, what is the expected uplift % (historical average from past similar promotions)?
Uplift learning: after each promotion ends, the system compares actual uplift to projected uplift. Over time, the model improves its uplift estimates per SKU and promotion type.
Scheme cost integration with the financial module (Module 5): when a promotion is added to the calendar, the revenue projection automatically nets out the scheme cost.
Cannibalisation flag: if a promotion on Model A historically pulls demand from Model B within the same family, the system flags this and suggests offsetting the Model B forecast downward.

  Module 9 â€” Exception-Based Planning & Alerts
9.1  The Problem It Solves
A planner managing 150 SKUs across 25 distributors cannot review every number every week. Exception-based planning inverts the process: instead of the planner hunting for problems, the system flags what needs attention. Planners spend their time resolving exceptions, not checking whether exceptions exist.
9.2  Exception Types and Alert Logic
Demand-Side Exceptions
Forecast bias alert: If actual tertiary is consistently above forecast for 3 consecutive weeks, the system flags 'Demand Uplift â€” Review Forecast Upward' for that SKU. Persistent below-forecast is flagged as 'Demand Softness.'
Pilot signal divergence: If pilot L7D average is growing rapidly while the national DRR is flat, flag as 'Emerging Demand â€” Pilot Outpacing National.' This is an early signal to increase weight on pilot in the blend.
New launch ramp anomaly: If a new launch is in week 6 and AO/SO is still below 30%, the launch activation is behind plan. Flag for immediate field action.
Supply-Side Exceptions
DOS floor breach imminent: Market DOS projected to cross below the configured floor (e.g. 10 days) within the next 3 weeks. Immediate action: increase primary order.
DOS ceiling breach imminent: Market DOS projected to exceed ceiling (e.g. 50 days) for 4+ weeks. Action: reduce dispatch, review if promotion is needed to accelerate sell-through.
Primary order vs forecast gap: If the confirmed order for a distributor is more than 30% above or below the S&OP forecast, flag for review â€” either the forecast is wrong or the distributor is gaming the system.
Inventory concentration risk: If more than 60% of a model's total market inventory is sitting with a single distributor, flag as concentration risk.
Process-Side Exceptions
Inputs not updated: If a model's PLC factor, DOS input, or weightages have not been reviewed in 4 weeks, flag as 'Stale Inputs.' Planning quality degrades when inputs are not maintained.
Consensus plan not ratified: If the weekly S&OP cycle is due but the consensus plan has not been approved, escalate to the S&OP manager.
Distributor order not placed: If it is day 22 of the month and a distributor has not placed their monthly order, flag for the ASM to follow up.
